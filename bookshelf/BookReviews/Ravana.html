Ravan Raja Rakshsancha

Sharad Tandale

“ज्याला इतिहासाने राक्षस म्हटले, तोसुद्धा कधी तरी माणूस होता.” ("The one whom history called a demon was once a human too.")

Ravana in Marathi literature (notably versions by authors like Anand Neelakantan and Ranjit Desai) retells the epic Ramayana from the perspective of its most misunderstood character — the demon king Ravana. Instead of portraying him as pure evil, the story delves into his intellect, devotion to Lord Shiva, and his struggle against destiny. Through rich narrative and layered emotions, this book humanizes Ravana and challenges the traditional idea of good versus evil.

⭐ Rating: 4.5 / 5 – A must-read for mythology and fantasy lovers.

This book is a bold and thought-provoking retelling that invites readers to look beyond the black-and-white morals of mythology. It portrays Ravana as a visionary, philosopher, and tragic hero. The Marathi language adds emotional depth and authenticity, giving the story a strong cultural resonance. Ideal for readers who enjoy reinterpreting classics, it’s a reminder that every villain has a story worth hearing.
