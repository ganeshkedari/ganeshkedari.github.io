Man's Search for Meaning

Viktor Frankl

4.9
Life-changing

"When we are no longer able to change a situation, we are challenged to change ourselves."

Viktor Frankl

Man's Search for Meaning is a profound book divided into two parts: Frankl's experiences as a prisoner in Nazi concentration camps and his psychotherapeutic method called logotherapy. Viktor Frankl, a Jewish psychiatrist who survived Auschwitz, chronicles the horrors of the Holocaust while exploring how some prisoners found reasons to live despite unimaginable suffering. He argues that the primary human drive is not pleasure but the pursuit of meaning. The second part explains logotherapy, which helps people discover meaning in their lives through creative work, loving relationships, or transforming unavoidable suffering. Written with deep humanity and wisdom, this book has sold millions of copies and been named one of the most influential books of all time.

This book shook me to my core. Reading about the Holocaust was hard, but Frankl writes with such dignity that it never feels exploitative. What struck me most was how he found meaning even in the darkest moments. The idea that we can choose our attitude in any situation is incredibly empowering, especially when life gets tough. I read it during a personal crisis and it helped me see my problems in perspective. Frankl does not preach or offer easy answers. He simply shows through his own life that suffering can have purpose if we choose to find it. This is one of those rare books that stays with you forever.
