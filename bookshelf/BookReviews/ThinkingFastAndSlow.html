Thinking, Fast and Slow

Daniel Kahneman

4.6
Groundbreaking

"Nothing in life is as important as you think it is when you are thinking about it."

Daniel Kahneman

Thinking, Fast and Slow explores the two systems that drive human thinking: System 1, which is fast, intuitive, and emotional, and System 2, which is slow, deliberate, and logical. Daniel Kahneman, a Nobel Prize-winning psychologist, spent decades researching how people make decisions and discovered that we rely too much on System 1, leading to biases and errors. The book covers cognitive biases like anchoring, availability heuristic, and overconfidence, showing how they affect everything from business decisions to everyday choices. Kahneman uses experiments and real-world examples to illustrate how our minds work and how we can make better decisions by engaging System 2. This groundbreaking work has influenced fields from economics to medicine and public policy.

This book completely changed how I see my own thinking. Kahneman shows you all the ways your brain tricks you without you even realizing it. The examples are fascinating and sometimes funny because you see yourself making the exact mistakes he describes. The book is long and can feel dense at times, but every chapter teaches you something valuable. I found the section on loss aversion especially interesting because it explained why I hold onto bad investments for too long. Reading this makes you more aware of your biases, which is the first step to making smarter decisions. It is a must read for anyone who wants to understand how their mind really works.
